1. Executive Summary

DUALFORCE is a 648 cc inline-two, four-stroke petrol engine with a linear reluctance electric assist built directly into the cylinders. A segmented coil stator sits outside each cylinder liner and pulls ferromagnetic teeth mounted on the piston skirt. The assist adds low-RPM torque, enables quiet low-speed urban commuting, and recovers braking energy without requiring an external, heavy traction motor or planetary reduction gearbox.

Status of the Evidence No physical prototype exists yet. Every number in this document is either (a) published data for the Royal Enfield 650 engine used as the fuel baseline, (b) a first-order engineering calculation with its explicit inputs shown, or (c) an estimate marked as such. Calculated figures are performance targets to be rigorously proven by the stage-gated test plan in Section 13, not certified test results.

What the Analysis Found

A rigorous first-order physics audit demonstrated that three design iterations were necessary to achieve commercial viability:

  • Iteration 1 (Original Catalog Concept): Cannot function as originally claimed. Squeezing 3,680 N of reluctance thrust across a wide gap into a cramped 150 mm² winding window requires an unviable current density of 52 A/mm², driving coil temperatures to 438°C and instantly failing the Class-220 insulation.
  • Iteration 2 (Thermal & Current Correction): Fixes the thermal runaway by enlarging the winding window to 350 mm² and derating flux to 1.0 T, keeping current density at a sustainable 10.7 A/mm² RMS and hot-spot temp at 133°C. However, with the 5.8 kWh battery, it only just holds 100 km/h on electric power with zero margin (drawing 122 Wh/km) and yields only ~63 km range at 60 km/h.
  • Iteration 3 (Production Target Design): Solves both range and top-speed targets by implementing: (1) bidirectional stator coils powering both up- and down-strokes, (2) stator-mounted Samarium-Cobalt (SmCo) bias magnets, (3) valvetrain deactivation reducing electric-mode drag to 0.08 kW per 1,000 rpm, and (4) an aerodynamic fairing (CdA 0.42 m²) with low-rolling-resistance tyres. Iteration 3 cruises at 100 km/h using only 47% available force and achieves 94–111 km range.

What Investors Are Funding

Investor capital is strictly targeted to prove four foundational engineering hypotheses:

  1. Force Feasibility: That ≥1,200 N per piston is achievable across a structural cylinder liner under acceptable excitation current.
  2. Thermal Survival: That Class-220 copper coils and stator-mounted SmCo magnets stay ≤150°C under continuous duty.
  3. Magnet-Bias Part-Load Efficiency: That stator bias magnets allow copper losses to scale down with the square of force at cruising loads.
  4. Net Torque & Range Payoff: That the assist delivers genuine net acceleration and range after deducting ring friction, valvetrain drag, and switching losses.

Capital is released strictly in stage-gated tranches tied to the test gates in Section 13.

2. Concept and Operating Modes

The engine retains a conventional forged crankshaft, connecting rods, and a six-speed manual gearbox. The electrical subsystem is a tubular switched-reluctance actuator wrapped concentrically around each cylinder liner. Because reluctance forces pull toward positions of lower magnetic reluctance, the stator is partitioned into independent coil groups energized synchronously with piston travel.

Operating Mode Electromechanical Operation Application Context
Electric Only Coils drive the pistons; fuel injection is cut off (0.0 L/h); valvetrain is decompressed. Low-speed city crawl (0–35 km/h), zero-emission zones.
Fuel Only Conventional 4-stroke combustion; stator coils idle with open H-bridge circuits. Steady-state open-highway cruising.
Electric + Fuel (Hybrid) Coils fire during 35° to 75° ATDC (maximum crank leverage) to superimpose torque on the combustion stroke. Overtaking maneuvers, high-speed launches, hill climb bursts.
Regenerative Braking Coils generate electrical power against piston reciprocating motion during deceleration. Braking and corner entry, recycling kinetic energy to battery.
Why This Architecture Is Challenging Three deeply coupled physics constraints govern feasibility:
1. The Magnetic Air Gap: The cylinder liner must simultaneously serve as a 75-bar pressure vessel and an electromagnetic window.
2. Copper Loss in Restricted Winding Volume: Packaging copper windings between structural cylinder bores limits cross-sectional area, making heat dissipation critical.
3. Electrical Conversion Efficiency: Unlike high-speed rotary BLDC motors (92–95% efficient), a linear reluctance engine must overcome reciprocating friction, valvetrain drag, and switching inductance.

5. Electromagnetic Subsystem: Calculations

5.1 First-Order Method & Governing Equations

1. Magnetic Pressure: p = B² / (2μ₀), where μ₀ = 1.2566 × 10⁻⁶ H/m.
2. Effective Magnetic Air Gap: g = t_liner + t_coating + clearance_piston.
3. Magnetomotive Force (MMF) per Coil: MMF = 2 · B · g / μ₀ (flux traverses gap twice).
4. Axial Reluctance Force per Tooth Pair: F = [B² / (2μ₀)] · g · π · D · k (where k ≈ 1.0).
5. Work Output per Stroke Event: W = F_total · Stroke · η_useful (useful fraction = 0.5).
6. Coil Resistance & Copper Loss: R = ρ_150C · L_wire / A_cu; Loss = I² · R.
7. Inductive Rise Time: L = N² / Reluctance; Δt_rise = L · I / V_bus.

5.2 Cylinder Liner Thickness & Pressure Containment

The effective magnetic gap cannot be 0.5 mm. The cylinder liner acts as a 75-bar combustion pressure vessel subject to hoop stress ($\sigma_h = \frac{P \cdot r}{t}$):

12. Competitive Position & Value Proposition

The rigorous baseline comparison is against a conventional motorcycle paired with a high-efficiency rotary permanent-magnet traction motor. Rotary motors achieve 90%+ electrical efficiency with mature automotive supply chains, but demand a separate motor casing, reduction gears, and external frame mounting (+35–45 kg penalty).

DUALFORCE trades peak electric efficiency for **unprecedented mechanical integration**: the engine block serves as the motor stator, and the piston crown serves as the rotor. The commercial thesis holds if and only if Gate G2 proves net torque additions after mechanical drag, and Gate G3 confirms that total powertrain packaging mass remains significantly below external hybrid systems.

SmCo Bias Magnets do not deliver F² loss law Electric range collapses from 94 km toward 63 km; misses target. Validated early in bench test T5. Fallback path: enlarge battery to 9.2 kWh or restrict electric mode to urban 0–40 km/h.
